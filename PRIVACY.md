**Publication evidence: 24 September 2026 UTC.** The [published policy](https://gist.github.com/aagrawal207/95acc71ba167fb754c435de17d256e59#file-privacy-md) was byte-verified at Gist revision `e84b3f6fba4d81425f5609196dc12fd59506a4ef`. [PUBLIC_PAGES.md](docs/app-store/PUBLIC_PAGES.md) owns publication records and [ROADMAP.md](docs/ROADMAP.md) owns round status.

This policy covers **Velvet Thorn** (App Store record: Breezy - Stream Music & Chill), developed by Abhishek. For privacy questions, contact **[aagrawal207@gmail.com](mailto:aagrawal207@gmail.com?subject=Velvet%20Thorn%20Privacy)**.

Velvet Thorn stores your library on your device and syncs shared library data through your iCloud Drive when available. Sync starts enabled on installations without a saved sync setting; you can turn it off in Settings. It contacts external services directly for music, artwork, lyrics, song credits, community skip markers and purchases; JioSaavn and Gaana can be switched off in Settings. Its companion shares limited playback and library data with your paired Apple Watch, and CarPlay displays the phone's music interface. It has no Velvet Thorn account service or app-operated analytics server. Provider requests leave the device, and device backups may include saved app data.

## Data saved on your device

Velvet Thorn's saved library includes:

- Liked, super liked and disliked songs, playlists and their names, followed artists, release updates, and identifiers for up to 60 pending release alerts. A super like is a stronger like kept inside the same library record; it changes only on-device ranking and the Download Liked Songs scope.
- Listening sessions and recent songs, with recording details, dates, and time listened. A history-reset identifier prevents pending listening from restoring history that you cleared or stopped saving.
- Lifetime listening receipts with a session UUID, cumulative seconds, counted-play flag, start date and recording-ID fingerprint. These preserve exact time/play totals after detailed history expires. They contain no title, artist or playable identifier; the fingerprint does not make listening data anonymous. At most 200,000 receipts fit in a history generation, and shared journal/file limits can bind sooner. Capacity exhaustion rejects further writes rather than silently dropping identities. Earlier deleted plays cannot be recovered.
- The queue, playback position and occurrence-order mapping, generated mixes, and preferences such as Home layout, accent, legacy discovery-language choices, each language's and each genre family's More/Automatic/Less recommendation adjustment, Shuffle order, Discovery balance, cache size, the player's right time label and swipe-up choice, Audio quality, Equalizer settings, Download over cellular, the Download Liked Songs scope (Off, Super Liked Only or All Liked), enabled catalog sources and Skip non-music sections.
- Up to 20 recently opened Search destinations, stored as recording or collection metadata with their type and provider identity. Explicit result, recent-item and result-menu destination choices update this list. Collection snapshots omit their track lists. Typing or submitting a query does not add an entry.
- Lyrics timing adjustments and manual song-language corrections.
- Verified tip transaction identifiers and whether each transaction is credited or revoked, to avoid counting the same purchase twice.

Search terms saved by older versions remain in a separate compatibility field until removed or search history is cleared. They are not converted into guessed songs or collections. Local liked-song/playlist filters are view state, not another saved search history.

The **Hold to Super Like** tip keeps its display state in Apple's TipKit datastore on this device: whether the tip has been shown and that a song was liked with the player open. It holds no song, account or library data, is not part of the library or iCloud sync, and is not sent to any service. Super likes and the tip make no network request of their own.

A few small surprises keep device-local flags in the app's standard user defaults: whether the 100-liked-songs celebration has been shown, the year of a dismissed listening-anniversary card on Home, and the time a sleep timer paused music while the app was in the background, kept until the app is next opened (the reminder appears only within 12 hours). They hold no song, account or library data, are not part of the library or iCloud sync, and are not sent to any service; normal device backups can include them. The anniversary card reads existing lifetime totals and retained history on this device and stays hidden while listening history is off. The short tabla sound on the About page is generated by the app and kept in purgeable Caches storage.

The library uses versioned atomic saves. If a saved file cannot be read safely, Velvet Thorn preserves it and shows an error rather than replacing it with an empty library. **Reload saved library** checks the saved file again and applies recovered preferences while keeping current playback. An untouched startup state can restore a recovered queue paused. Failed reloads preserve the last safely loaded library.

Listening time that cannot be saved remains in a bounded memory buffer during the current app session. Playback pauses if that buffer fills. This unsaved time can be lost when the app closes; it is discarded when a successful history-clear or history-disable change requires it. The buffer is not a second persistent history file.

Fewer repeats reads counted plays from the last seven days when a new shuffle order is requested. History off or unavailable saved storage supplies no evidence, so ordering is random while the chosen setting remains saved. Discovery balance ranks existing candidate pools using saved listening/library evidence. These controls maintain no separate persistent listening counter; the shuffle occurrence mapping belongs only to device-local playback state. The preference choices sync through iCloud when enabled.

Audio and artwork use separate temporary caches. The audio cache can hold several immutable encodings of the same recording, with codec, bitrate, duration, byte count, preparation version, storage time and optional display metadata such as title, credits, album and artwork URL. It does not save signed streaming URLs. Older sidecars can use matching metadata already in the saved library, without fetching missing names for the inventory. Lyrics text, album descriptions, temporary catalog responses and resolved stream links use in-memory caches. There is no separate persistent album-description store.

Downloads use a permanent tier at `Application Support/GanaBajana/Downloads`, outside cache limits and excluded from backup and library sync. A complete cached file can move there without another transfer. Remove Download returns it to the cache; Delete removes it, retaining an in-use copy through the existing reader rules.

The background queue in `downloads-queue.json` stores recording IDs, operation tokens, attempt/phase/retry information and URL-free acquisition fields, including the actual playback provider when known. Cache sidecars retain that same provider value; it is not inferred from signed URLs. The queue stores neither signed URLs nor URLSession resume data. iOS retains the request URL, including signed parameters, inside its background task. Submitted transfers can continue during suspension; extraction and validation wait for foreground runtime. Force-quitting stops transfers until reopening. Token-named delivery files and receipts in `DownloadStaging` are excluded from backup and sync. [Background Downloads](docs/BACKGROUND_DOWNLOADS.md) owns limits and recovery.

**Export Audio** reads a complete local song and creates verified tagged AAC-LC M4A in a unique `tmp/Export` directory. Existing AAC can be preserved; other supported audio is encoded locally using Apple frameworks. The original is unchanged. Title, artist, available album and cover bytes are checked through native readback. Artwork can be fetched through the existing loader; a known artwork URL that cannot be loaded produces a retryable error rather than a coverless export. A genuinely missing URL needs no request. The system share sheet receives the M4A file and verified cover preview, not a second image attachment. Export does not fetch audio or upload it to a service. Unretained exports older than one hour are eligible for pruning; active exports and retained shares are protected. Copies sent to Files or other apps are outside Velvet Thorn's control, and those apps may ignore cover metadata.

**Live Radio** favorites are saved separately in `Application Support/GanaBajana/live-radio-favorites.json`, with at most 200 station entries. Each contains public directory metadata, including the public stream URL, rather than a song record or a resolved redirect target. Favorites do not sync through the library but can enter device backups. Listening to a station adds no song history, recent song, download or cache file. The saved music queue and position remain separate and return paused.

**Song and Artist Radio** keeps up to 20 recent starting songs/artists and their tuning in `Application Support/GanaBajana/radio-history.json` for Listen Again. This device-local file can enter device backups but does not sync through iCloud library replicas. Turning Save Listening History off or clearing listening history removes these recent choices. A failed library load does not erase the radio file. Radio searches and original-seed recommendation requests go to the catalog provider; the complete recent-radio list and local tuning are not uploaded as a new history payload.

**Skip and completion signals** record how each song you listened to ended, so recommendations can play fewer songs you skip early. Each signal holds the recording ID, title, artist names, the end time, the seconds you listened, the song length, whether you skipped or finished it, whether you or Velvet Thorn chose it, and whether it was part of a quick run of skips. They are kept in `Application Support/GanaBajana/listening-signals.json` on this device: at most 2,048 signals from the last 90 days, in a file of at most 1 MiB. The file is excluded from backups and is not part of the library or iCloud sync. Nothing is recorded while Save Listening History is off. Turning it off or clearing listening history erases the signals. A failed library load does not erase the file, and an unreadable file is kept and stops new recording. Their logs carry only fixed categories and counts, never titles or identifiers.

**Extractor Updates** stores validated compatibility packages, a revision floor and rollback choices in backup-excluded `Application Support/GanaBajana/ExtractorUpdates/state.json`. It stores no player script, media URL, challenge, account data or imported Files path. Manual import reads the selected file and requires explicit source trust for unsigned packages. Rules support bounded field aliases and hash-matched transforms, not arbitrary code or automatically generated repairs. No feed or publisher key is configured, so no update server is contacted. [Extractor Updates](docs/EXTRACTOR_UPDATES.md) describes the optional feed mechanism and recovery.

**Moment Mixes** learns coarse time/day and explicit route contexts on this device. Its separate backup-excluded `Moments/moments.json` holds at most 2,048 qualified event references within 60 days, context switches and bounded daily recording selections. It contains no coordinates, route names, hardware IDs or raw motion samples. History preparation runs in a detached task; generation and privacy checks reject stale results. Learning defaults on; optional motion defaults off and requests permission only when enabled in Settings. History Off hides learned cards and stops capture. Clear History retires evidence and selections while preserving explicit switches; a failed erase remains blocked and retryable under the library privacy epoch. No Moments data enters iCloud library sync, widgets or companions. Generation makes no provider requests, though artwork and playback keep their normal network behavior.

Song details are cached on this device under `Caches/Details` as small JSON files (at most 500, oldest removed first) holding the credits, release facts, identifiers, view count and description fetched for a recording. The folder is purgeable and separate from the synced library. Genre evidence is kept in `Application Support/GanaBajana/genres.json`: for each recording you played long enough to count, and for each MusicBrainz artist it looked up, the genre families that MusicBrainz's tags mapped to, with check, expiry and last-use dates (at most 5,000 recordings and 2,000 artists). It stores no tags, titles, listening events or play counts, is not synced, and is deleted when you clear listening history. SponsorBlock skip markers are kept in memory only and vanish when the app closes.

If you import your YouTube Music library, a small ledger at `Application Support/GanaBajana/youtube-import.json` records the date of the last import, how many items each part imported, and which of your local playlists came from which account playlist, so a later import adds new songs to the same playlist instead of creating a copy. It holds no credentials, song lists or account name, is not synced, and stays when you sign out.

Listening-language discovery also uses a bounded, expiring local cache. It stores a recording ID and normalized matching metadata, the language result and its provenance, and check/expiry times. Provenance can identify YouTube audio-language metadata or MusicBrainz recording/work IDs. It stores no lyric text, audio, listening events or aggregate play counts. Identified results expire after 12 hours; unclassified results use a shorter lifetime. This derived file is purgeable, excluded from backups on successful writes and separate from the synced library. Saved recording corrections and explicit recommendation adjustments remain in the library.

For eligible Opus recordings on supported iOS versions, Velvet Thorn downloads a complete WebM file and uses the bundled FFmpegBridge to copy its encoded audio into an MP4 container locally. Apple's media framework checks native decoded audio before the prepared file becomes playable. FFmpegBridge has no network or audio-decoder functionality and does not send audio to an additional service. Preparation uses temporary files and memory; the source preparation file is removed when the operation finishes or fails. A validated output can remain in the purgeable audio cache alongside an existing encoding.

Online Search results update while you type, so partial text can be sent before you submit it even though query text is not added to Recently opened. Liked-song/playlist filtering uses local metadata. Known-offline Search searches saved metadata rather than sending a catalog search.

Opening the player's artist-choice list can request the credited artist IDs, or search their names, and download provider-supplied pictures before an artist is selected. Optional pictures use bounded in-memory lookup caches and the existing artwork cache. Selection remains available while these requests run.

Velvet Thorn prepares songs you are likely to play next before you press Play: up to the next five in your queue and the first few on Search, Home, album and artist pages. It looks up their audio links and reads the start of each file; prepared audio stays in memory. The song you are playing, and the next one once you have listened for about 30 seconds, are downloaded completely into the removable cache. Browsing pages prepare nothing on cellular unless **Download over cellular** is on, and nothing is prepared in Low Data Mode. These requests can use your network connection, including cellular data when iOS permits it. Opening an artist page sends bounded song searches to the catalog provider to find collaborations for its **Featuring [artist]** section. Opening a **This is [artist]** popular-song page can trigger bounded lyrics and configured MusicBrainz requests even while **Languages** is collapsed and the lyrics sheet is closed.

After qualifying listening, Velvet Thorn can also check recording languages automatically while the app is active, listening-history saving is enabled and the device is not known offline. It first reuses cached results or checks the recording's default audio-language tag through the YouTube Data API. A usable tag avoids lyrics lookup and recognition. Missing tags can lead to recording-matching requests to LRCLIB and configured MusicBrainz, with linked YouTube Music lyrics as fallback, without opening an artist page or lyrics sheet. Home and the Listening languages page can request another bounded pass. Pausing the app, known-offline status or disabling history retires these automatic checks; previously saved cache evidence may still be read for existing history.

Language recognition from matched lyrics runs on your phone. The YouTube metadata response uses a bounded memory cache and can supply a missing duration to a temporary lyrics/MusicBrainz request; it does not rewrite the saved track or playback position. The conditional work lookup sends a MusicBrainz entity ID, not lyric text. Manual corrections are saved in your library and are not submitted to Google or MusicBrainz. Language-browser expansion and filter selection are kept only in memory.

Album descriptions use Wikipedia text already supplied in the selected YouTube Music album header. An optional description-only lookup sends the album browse ID to the existing catalog endpoint and can use its normal bootstrap request. The article and supplied license links are retained, including CC BY-SA 3.0 when supplied. This feature adds no automatic Wikipedia, Wikidata, or MusicBrainz request, and requires no additional account or authentication.

**Reload best available audio** requests fresh source selection and may download a new encoding when a connection is available. Known-offline resolution uses compatible local files, including on a forced retry; the quality-reload control is disabled in that state. The resulting prepared cache variant can be reused on later plays; it does not upload your cached library. Song/artist Radio sends the chosen search text, artist ID or recording seed to the provider. Tune Radio applies familiarity and recording-language evidence locally to future recommendations.

Personal mix ranking, recap and language-share calculations run on the phone. Automatic discovery uses qualified listening and saved language adjustments to choose recording seeds and language search text. Provider requests for candidates, followed artists and radio seeds can therefore reveal interests derived from your library. The complete local library is not uploaded to a Velvet Thorn server.

Genre learning works the same way as language learning. After a song counts as played, Velvet Thorn can ask MusicBrainz which genres its community tagged the recording, its release group or its artists with, while the app is active, listening history is on and the device is not known offline, at most 60 recordings per app session and never within the first ten seconds after launch. MusicBrainz receives the same title, artist and duration search and entity identifiers it already receives for language checks, never your listening history. The genre families shown in Recap, Settings > Discovery > Listening genres and Song details are computed on the phone from that evidence, and the More/Automatic/Less genre adjustments change recommendations only. Home shelves, Daily Mix, Discover Weekly and the radio station order can reflect those genres, so the seeds and searches they generate can reveal genre interests as well as language interests.

**Song details** reads the credits, release facts, identifiers and description of the playing song. YouTube's own upload description is read first through the Data API or the fallback player request above; MusicBrainz is asked only for gaps. Recordings from JioSaavn or Gaana are looked up on MusicBrainz only. The description is provider text about a public upload, shown as received and never sent anywhere. Copying the recording ID writes it to the system pasteboard on your device.

**Skip non-music sections** is off by default. When on, the community markers for a playing YouTube song are fetched as described above and the player seeks past marked talk, intros, outros and sponsor reads. The skipped stretch is not counted as listening. Markers are not saved to disk or synced.

**Catalog Sources** controls JioSaavn and Gaana visibility in Search. Saved songs still route to their provider; cross-source playback fallback and the additional JioSaavn lyric source have their own matching paths. Turning off Search visibility does not disable every provider request. YouTube Music cannot be turned off.

Lyric variant discovery uses bounded LRCLIB, recording-linked catalog and JioSaavn lookups. Published text, source credits and up to 32 remembered choices stay in memory. Recording corrections take priority over supplied language and the Data API's raw audio-language code. Gurmukhi is offered only when published text exists; no translation or transliteration is generated. There is no direct Genius or Musixmatch client. [Lyric Variants](docs/LYRICS_VARIANTS.md) records request and memory bounds.

**TV Lyrics** shares the selected lyric document and source text with an opt-in phone view or an iOS-provided external display. Screen Mirroring is a system action. Ordinary audio AirPlay sends no app-owned lyric screen. TV presentation makes no additional provider or artwork request. Stop clears presentation; phone inactivity blanks it, backgrounding stops it, and privacy reset or sign-out intent clears selection. Lyrics and display state are not saved or synced.

## iCloud sync

**iCloud sync defaults on when this device has no saved sync setting.** A saved Off choice stays off. Manage it through **Settings > Library & iCloud > iCloud sync**. When enabled and iCloud Drive is available, shared library records are sent to the app's container in your iCloud Drive. They include likes, super likes and dislikes, playlists and names, followed artists and release metadata, listening sessions and recents, typed Search selections and retained legacy queries, generated mixes, saved recording metadata and artwork URLs, manual language corrections, lyric timing, and all saved preferences. Preferences include Home layout, app icon, appearance, legacy discovery choices, independent recommendation adjustments for each language and each genre family, playback controls, the player's time label and swipe-up choices, Audio quality, Equalizer settings, Download over cellular, the Download Liked Songs scope, enabled catalog sources, Skip non-music sections, cache-size choice, DNS and remote-fallback choices. Songs, playlists and follows imported from a YouTube Music account are ordinary library records and sync like any other edit.

Lifetime receipts sync with library history, joining matching sessions by maximum cumulative seconds and counted-play state. Clear History advances the same reset generation so stale replicas cannot restore totals. Rankings and language/genre shares still use retained detail, not lifetime receipts.

The app also stores merge revisions, installation identifiers, deletion markers and reset/compaction boundaries. Current queue/position, pending notification deliveries, tips, tip display state, credentials, media, download queue/staging, import ledger, live-radio favorites, extractor packages, account-write permission, Moments file, lyric choices/TV state and derived caches are excluded from library sync. Language/genre shares and cache scores are calculated locally. Apple handles cloud storage; Velvet Thorn does not receive your Apple Account password.

A device-local configuration stores the opaque iCloud account binding, installation replica identifier, sync enablement and last-success time. This configuration is excluded from backups, so a restored installation without it starts with sync enabled and a fresh identity. On an installation with an existing account binding, a different Apple Account disables sync until you explicitly enable it for that account. Turning sync off, signing out or deleting the app keeps existing iCloud files; offline changes are merged if sync resumes.

Item removals and history/search clears propagate to other devices when they sync. Unresolved historical iCloud file versions are retained, so clearing data in Velvet Thorn does not physically purge those versions or existing device backups. Manage Apple's retained copies through the applicable iCloud and backup controls. The developer does not operate a library-storage server or receive the synced library.

## Home Screen widgets

Velvet Thorn publishes a bounded copy of saved song titles, artist names, recording identifiers, liked-song counts and generated-mix previews to a shared container on this device. A super liked song appears there as an ordinary liked song. It includes small artwork images already cached by the app. This derived copy is separate from the saved library, excluded from iCloud sync and excluded from backups on successful writes.

The widget extension reads this copy without contacting music, artwork or account services. It receives no audio, signed media URLs, account credentials or tip records. Tapping a widget opens Velvet Thorn, whose ordinary playback and provider behavior still applies. Personal widget content is marked sensitive for the system's privacy presentation.

Turning listening history off hides recent songs on the widget; clearing history removes its recents and generated mixes. The app replaces the shared projection and requests a widget refresh after a durable change. iOS controls when the displayed widget updates, so an earlier rendering may remain visible until the refresh occurs.

Lock Screen widgets and Controls read a separate small file in the same shared container: whether something is selected, whether it is playing, and the song title and artist or the station name. It carries no identifiers, URLs, positions or queue. While listening history is off, the title and artist are left out and the widget shows a generic Resume. Playing, pausing or opening the player from a widget or Control runs inside Velvet Thorn, not in the extension.

## Apple Watch and CarPlay

When the Watch companion is installed, Apple's WatchConnectivity framework exchanges data with the paired iPhone. Playback snapshots include the recording ID, title, artist, position, duration, transport state, favorite/shuffle/repeat settings, and fixed error messages; a super like is reported as an ordinary favorite. Requested library pages include playlist names and song counts, and bounded rows from Favorites, Recently played, Playlists, and Queue. Watch actions send control choices and item identifiers back to the iPhone.

These Watch payloads contain no audio, signed media URLs, account cookies, or artwork/media assets. The Watch has no catalog, sign-in, or audio-streaming client. The iPhone owns playback and saves library changes. WatchConnectivity can retain the latest background snapshot for later delivery; browsed pages stay in Watch app memory rather than a second saved library. Disconnected or unconfirmed data can remain visible as **Last seen on iPhone** until refreshed.

CarPlay displays library rows and the phone's existing system Now Playing metadata through native templates. Its controls use the same iPhone player and audio route. CarPlay list artwork uses system symbols without additional image downloads. Watch and CarPlay controls can cause the phone's ordinary playback/provider requests, with the same optional account behavior described below. They add no external music provider or Velvet Thorn server.

## Handoff and nearby devices

**Handoff.** While music plays, the app offers Apple's Handoff with a small description of the session: the song IDs, titles, artists, durations and artwork links for the current song and up to 160 around it, the position, shuffle and repeat, and whether it was playing. Apple delivers it to your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account, as with any Handoff activity. It never includes audio, signed media links, account cookies or listening history, and it is not offered during Live Radio. When another device picks it up, this device pauses.

**Devices.** The Devices screen finds Velvet Thorn on your other iPhone, iPad or Mac on the same local network. iOS and macOS ask for Local Network access when you first open it, and you can turn Find Nearby Devices off at any time. Each device advertises a random name that changes every launch; device names, what is playing and controls travel only inside an encrypted connection between your own devices. Moving playback sends the queue (song IDs, titles, artists, artwork links), the position and whether it was playing. Nothing passes through a Velvet Thorn server.

The connection key is stored in your Keychain and syncs through iCloud Keychain when it is on, which is how your devices recognise each other. If iCloud Keychain is off, a one-time 16-character pairing code shown on one device and entered on the other shares the key directly. Reset Device Key replaces it.

## Optional YouTube sign-in

Signing in is optional. An explicit Welcome completion offers Connect YouTube or Not Now once; relaunch and previously completed libraries skip that offer. Only tapping Connect opens sign-in, and signing in never starts an import automatically. The sign-in sheet loads Google's page at `accounts.google.com` and related resources. Google receives the information entered there and associated browser/session data.

After sign-in, Velvet Thorn retains cookies scoped to YouTube domains, their scope/expiry information, sign-in time and a random sign-in identifier used to bind account-write permission. These cookies are authentication credentials. They are stored separately from the library in the device Keychain using an after-first-unlock, device-only protection setting. Velvet Thorn does not save a separate account-name field, email field or password. The sign-in web view uses a non-persistent data store.

The saved session serves YouTubeKit's TVHTML5 playback lookup, explicit account-library import and separately enabled account changes described below. It is not added to ordinary catalog, chart, artwork, YouTube Data API, LRCLIB, MusicBrainz, JioSaavn, Gaana, SponsorBlock, Song details, linked-lyrics, radio or extractor-feed requests. Google can associate authenticated requests with your account. Velvet Thorn does not deliberately send watch-history updates or scrobbles, but Google can retain or use request data under its own policies.

### Importing your YouTube Music library

**Settings > Account & connection > Import from YouTube Music** appears only while you are signed in. It copies liked songs, playlists, followed artists and, if you turn it on, recent history from the signed-in account into this device's library. Nothing is changed on the account: Velvet Thorn only reads the account's listings, and re-running the import adds what is new without unliking, unfollowing or removing anything here. Google can see these requests on your account. The import sends your saved session with each listing request to `music.youtube.com`; it reads at most 2,000 liked songs, 200 playlists of up to 1,000 songs each, 500 followed artists and the most recent 200 unique plays, and never caches the account pages. Imported history becomes a playlist named "YouTube Music history"; it does not add listening events, recent songs or recap minutes. A local ledger (described above) remembers which local playlists came from the account and survives sign-out, so signing in later resumes updating the same playlists.

Use **Settings > Account & connection > Sign out** to remove the saved session. Velvet Thorn clears its in-process session and saves a small, credential-free sign-out marker in the app's local data before deleting the Keychain entry. A successfully saved marker prevents that session from being restored after relaunch if deletion fails. An unreadable sign-out status also blocks restoration.

**Sign-out pending** means another step is needed, not that credentials have necessarily been deleted. **Retry sign-out** retries deletion and marker cleanup. If the app says sign-out could not be saved, retry before closing: without a saved marker, the old session may return on relaunch. Device-only Keychain credentials do not migrate to another device, and Keychain items may survive app deletion.

### Changing your YouTube account

**Allow YouTube Changes** is off by default. Settings > Account & Connection > YouTube Changes enables it only for the current sign-in on this device. Older saved sign-ins need reconnecting before it can be enabled. Each remote action still requires confirmation. Eligible songs offer Like on YouTube, Remove YouTube Like and Add to YouTube Playlist; exact linked channels offer Subscribe and Unsubscribe. Local hearts, playlists, follows, imports and iCloud merges remain separate and do not automatically change YouTube.

The client uses the default Google account. Multiple-account and brand-account selection are unsupported, and Velvet Thorn has no verified account name to display. Only playlists with current provider ownership evidence can receive a song. Google receives the selected IDs and authenticated request data and may refuse the unofficial connection.

A separate device-only, non-iCloud Keychain record stores permission, its sign-in binding and a random change generation, without credentials or URLs. New sign-ins cannot reuse old permission. Sign-out, disabling changes or leaving the page cancels pending work. Failed permission removal stays disabled in memory and displays a retry-before-closing error because an older permission record may still be durable. Device-only Keychain items do not migrate and may survive app deletion.

Only one account operation is active at a time. Mutations have no automatic retries, offline queue or reconnect replay. A lost response or cancellation can leave the outcome uncertain because Google may already have accepted the change. Velvet Thorn asks you to check YouTube Music before trying again. Provider responses and playlist selections are kept only for the active workflow, not saved as an account mirror. [Account Write-Back](docs/ACCOUNT_WRITEBACK.md) owns exact request and retention bounds.

## Optional remote playback fallback

**Remote playback fallback is off by default.** If you enable it in Settings and local extraction fails, YouTubeKit contacts `remote-production.youtubekit.dev` with the recording ID and, when available, an app-identity header derived from the signed app.

That service can ask the phone to make additional extraction requests to service-selected URLs. It receives the resulting response URLs, status codes, headers, and bodies, including intermediate responses when requested. Extraction transport disables automatic cookie and credential storage, but service-directed or returned headers may still contain cookies. Velvet Thorn's account bridge does not add your saved YouTube session to this remote path. Response-size limits and cancellation do not determine the remote service's retention or data use, which have not been independently established.

## Encrypted DNS

**Encrypted DNS is enabled by default** and can be changed in Settings. It keeps the app's hostname lookups for music sites private from the local network, such as public Wi-Fi. Velvet Thorn prefers the system's encrypted DNS or Private Relay and configures Cloudflare's `cloudflare-dns.com/dns-query` as a fallback. The selected resolver receives hostname queries and connection metadata.

This setting applies to new app connections that use the system networking context. It does not change device-wide DNS and does not hide music requests from their providers.

## Purchases

Tips are optional, one-time consumable purchases through Apple and unlock no features or music. Apple handles payment details; Velvet Thorn does not receive your payment-card number.

The app verifies StoreKit transactions and keeps a local transaction-ID record, including credit or revocation state, before acknowledging them. The record supports duplicate prevention and the tip count shown on this device. It is part of the saved library and may be included in device backups. It is not a subscription or a cross-device entitlement. Billing and refund requests are handled by Apple at [reportaproblem.apple.com](https://reportaproblem.apple.com/).

## Notifications and diagnostics

New-release alerts use local iOS notifications after permission is granted. The app checks followed artists when opened and when iOS schedules background refresh. Pending alert identifiers are saved with release data so eligible alerts can retry after relaunch or a failed delivery, including before the next catalog check is due. Stable notification identifiers help avoid duplicates. Pending records are limited to retained releases; delivery is not guaranteed.

Turning off **New release alerts** or unfollowing an artist removes the corresponding pending records when the change saves. This does not withdraw notifications already submitted to iOS. Alert content can include artist and release names on the Lock Screen, subject to your notification settings. There is no Velvet Thorn push-notification server.

Velvet Thorn writes diagnostic messages through Apple's local logging system. These can include operation outcomes, timings, provider identifiers, and error details. The app has no custom remote analytics or crash-reporting endpoint. Apple may process diagnostics according to your device's analytics and sharing settings.

Song-language logs contain fixed outcome counters and timings, without lyric text, song titles, artist names, or entity IDs. Development tests can inspect bounded numerical language evidence without lyric text; that test-only inspection is excluded from release builds.

Listening-discovery, typed Search-selection, connectivity and cache records use fixed categories, numeric counts/status values and bounded outcomes. They do not add song, user, query or request-ID dimensions. Cache read failure is distinct from an empty cache, and a derived-cache write failure is distinct from saved evidence.

Album-description logs contain local outcomes and timings, without prose, album IDs, artist names or URLs. CarPlay logs local template and navigation failures. Download, export, Song details, catalog-source, SponsorBlock, equalizer, normalization, genre, import, account-change, radio, extractor-update and hidden-delight logs use counts, byte sizes and bounded outcomes. These feature events contain no titles, recording/playlist/station identifiers, queries, URLs, challenges, publisher key IDs or account data. They add no remote analytics endpoint.

## Retention, deletion, and backups

- Listening history saving defaults on. Successfully turning off **Save listening history** stops new listening events, recent-song records and automatic listening-derived language checks, and discards pending listening. Existing history and explicit adjustments remain. It does not disable requests caused by browsing/playback, typed Search-history saving or queue-position saving.
- **Clear Listening History** removes sessions, lifetime receipts, recents and generated mixes, resets derived language evidence and deletes genre evidence. It also retires Moment evidence/selections and TV presentation. Cleanup failures remain visible and retryable. Explicit adjustments, recording corrections and Moment switches stay saved. Detail normally has a 400-day retention window; a current streak may keep one older session per day. Lifetime time/plays survive detail pruning within the exact receipt, shared journal and file limits, but Clear removes them too.
- **Search > Recently opened** offers individual removal and **Clear all**. A successful search-history clear also removes retained legacy terms and advances the shared reset generation. Clearing listening history does not clear either search-history field.
- **Settings > Library & iCloud > Storage** shows actual usage and offers cache clearing. Its audio-byte total includes every committed encoding and active preparation files; the song count counts distinct recordings. All encodings of audio currently in use may be retained, and caches can refill during use. iOS can purge caches independently. Downloads are listed separately with their own count and size; they are not counted in the cache limit, not removed by cache clearing and not purged by iOS.
- Cached songs offers per-recording removal across its audio variants. Complete audio has no time-based expiry; byte/song capacity and a local score derived from durable listening guide eviction. No additional listening history is recorded while history saving is off. Cache-access times remain a fallback ordering signal. Artwork, lyrics and audio have separate availability.
- Downloads stay until Remove Download, Delete or app deletion. Unliking, demoting a super like or narrowing the Download Liked Songs scope never deletes a file; the All/Super Liked/Liked/Not Liked filters only change the visible list. Unretained exports older than one hour are pruned; iOS may clear temporary files. Shared copies are outside Velvet Thorn's control. The Song details cache is purgeable and holds at most 500 recordings; the import ledger stays until app deletion.
- Likes, super likes, playlists, follows, preferences, corrections, and tip records persist until removed through their available controls or the local app data is deleted. Cache/history clearing does not erase the tip ledger or the tip display state.
- Radio favorites stay until removed individually or app data is deleted; clearing song history or signing out does not remove them. Normal backups can include the favorites file. Extractor state is excluded from backups/library sync and keeps its revision floor through rollback or Use Bundled Rules. Account-write permission stays in device-only Keychain storage, outside preferences and backups.
- Normal iOS backups may include the library and its sync journal, preferences, history/reset generations, typed Search selections and retained legacy terms, corrections, pending alert identifiers, tip identifiers, the credential-free sign-out marker, the pending download queue, the import ledger and the genre evidence file. Depending on your backup settings, those copies may be stored in iCloud or on a computer. Downloaded audio, device-only Keychain credentials and the excluded device-sync configuration do not migrate with them. iCloud library sync is separate from these backups. The paired Watch receives the limited display data described above. Temporary audio/artwork, Song details and derived language caches use purgeable Caches storage.
- Deleting the app removes its local app-container data. It does not delete existing backups, Apple purchase records, provider-held records, or necessarily Keychain items. Sign out before app deletion if you want to remove the saved YouTube session, and manage backups through iOS or your backup software.

## YouTube API Services

Velvet Thorn uses YouTube API Services (the YouTube Data API) to read a recording's default audio language and, when missing, its duration. By using Velvet Thorn you agree to be bound by the [YouTube Terms of Service](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms); Google's handling of the data it receives is described in the [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy). These requests carry Velvet Thorn's app credential and bundle identifier, not your Google account: Velvet Thorn requests no Google account authorization for the API, so there is no authorized access to revoke in your Google account settings. Google receives the recording IDs it looks up, along with the connection details described above. The returned metadata is kept in a bounded memory cache for up to 12 hours (missing recordings for 15 minutes), which ends with the app process, and its language result can enter the expiring local language cache described earlier, which **Clear listening history** or deleting the app removes. It is not shared with other services. Velvet Thorn adds no advertising to YouTube content and stores no YouTube API data about you beyond that cache.
